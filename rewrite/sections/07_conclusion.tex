\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Synchronized fresh inversions across non-U.S. G10 yield curves predict a distinct currency-return state. Annualized carry-trade spot returns are 12.9 percentage points lower when the lagged state is active, and total carry returns fall from 6.5 to $-4.9$ percent. The known interest differential remains positive; exchange-rate losses overwhelm it. Median, quantile, and trimmed estimates show that the result is broader than a few crash months.

The cross-section supplies the economic interpretation. High-rate currencies lose relative to low-rate funding currencies, and bilateral losses become larger as predetermined equity-market beta rises. Persistent portfolio membership, a 0.72 alignment between rate and beta rankings, and a beta-sorted portfolio loss show that the state reprices a standing currency characteristic rather than merely rotating the carry portfolio. Directionally similar emerging-market responses extend the pattern beyond currencies used to construct the state, although they are not an independent replication.

This ordering supports the interpretation that synchronized inversions contain information about a shared international risk rather than only separate national disturbances. The evidence does not identify whether that information concerns global growth, coordinated policy, inflation, term premia, intermediary capacity, or a mixture. Slowly adjusting compensation is one coherent explanation, not a recovered structural mechanism.

The estimates use fifteen historical episodes and have not been evaluated in a frozen out-of-sample window. A prospective test should fix the state algorithm and data-vintage protocol before the next synchronized episode occurs.
