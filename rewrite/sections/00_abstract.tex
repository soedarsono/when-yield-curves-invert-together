Currencies face country-specific and global risks. An inversion of one country's yield curve may reflect either, but synchronized inversions across several non-U.S. G10 economies are more likely to contain information about global conditions. I combine these inversions into a state variable that predicts unusually poor carry-trade spot returns. Annualized carry-trade spot returns are 12.9 percentage points lower when the lagged state is active than when inactive, with inference based on fifteen episodes. High-interest-rate currencies and currencies with greater predetermined equity-market betas experience larger losses, supporting the interpretation that the state contains information about global rather than country-specific risk.
