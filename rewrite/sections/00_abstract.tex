National yield curves mix country-specific and shared information. I use their cross-country configuration to construct a predictive state: fresh 10-year/2-year inversions must be live in at least two non-U.S. G10 economies. From 1988 to 2026, annualized carry-trade spot returns are 12.9 percentage points lower when the lagged state is active, with inference based on fifteen episodes. Separate exactly-one and exactly-two state coefficients have opposite signs, and the configuration retains predictive content alongside the U.S. curve, the average G10 slope, and individual-country states. Losses fall on the high-interest-rate currencies bought by carry trades and grow with predetermined S\&P 500 equity beta. The precise carry estimate depends on tenor and release design, while the exposure ordering is more stable. Cross-country yield-curve configurations therefore reveal a predictive state for exposure-ordered currency losses.
