\section{The Cross-Country Configuration}
\label{sec:main_evidence}

\subsection{Conditional carry returns}

Carry spot returns are 12.85 percentage points per year lower when the lagged synchronized-inversion state is active. They average 2.85 percent in 366 inactive months and $-10.00$ percent in 92 active months. The money-market-differential total-return proxy falls from 6.45 to $-4.86$ percent, a difference of $-11.31$ points. Episode-bootstrap $p$-values are 0.001 and 0.003.

The accounting locates the result. The carry income leg averages 3.91 percent per year and is positive in every month, with a minimum of 0.7 percent. It does not fall enough to create the conditional loss; the spot depreciation overwhelms it. The object is therefore a predictable spot-risk state and, under the money-market accounting, a negative conditional total-return proxy. Executable forward quotes, basis, spreads, and financing costs are not observed over the full sample.

The distribution shifts with the mean. The monthly carry-spot median changes from 0.35 percent outside the state to $-0.76$ percent inside it. The 25th percentile moves from $-0.93$ to $-1.98$ percent and the 10th percentile from $-2.35$ to $-3.94$ percent. Expected shortfall worsens from $-4.80$ to $-8.14$ percent. The conditional variance ratio is 1.74, with permutation $p=0.14$: the evidence is stronger for a leftward shift than for a general variance increase.

Deleting each series' full-sample bottom-decile months leaves carry spot and total differences of $-7.24$ and $-5.50$ points, with episode-bootstrap $p=0.006$ and 0.024. This deletion uses realized outcomes and is not a preferred estimator. Its narrower purpose is clear: a small set of worst observations does not arithmetically generate the entire conditional mean.

\subsection{Exactly-one and exactly-two indicator regressions}

The most revealing comparison is inside the count. In 88 months with exactly one live inversion, carry spot and total coefficients are $+8.33$ and $+8.78$ percentage points per year, with $p=0.005$ and 0.003. In 48 months with exactly two, the coefficients reverse to $-15.72$ and $-15.13$, with $p=0.001$ and 0.002. The S\&P-beta portfolio shows the same qualitative break: $+0.69$ after exactly one and $-10.43$ after exactly two.

The opposite coefficient signs are the economic content of synchronization. A lone fresh inversion can arise from one country's rate cycle, while a diversified carry portfolio continues to collect the cross-country interest differential. At the second curve, the measured configuration changes. Because each coefficient compares its indicated months with a different complement, these regressions do not estimate a direct exactly-one-versus-exactly-two contrast. They nevertheless show why an “any inversion” label can combine return associations with opposite signs. The estimates do not make two a universal structural threshold.

Counts above two do not produce a monotone dose response. At least three live curves gives a $-6.77$-point carry-spot coefficient, and at least four gives $-0.75$; both are imprecise and use fewer months. These states also occur later within episodes. The nested at-least-two rule retains the configuration after additional curves enter; the exact-two row is the sharper historical cell, not a stable rule that switches off when a third curve inverts.

\subsection{The reported state-definition neighborhood}

Figure~\ref{fig:v03-reported-neighborhood} displays the nine unique breadth, release, and tenor rules reported in the analysis. The carry estimate has two load-bearing measurement margins. Releasing after one increase leaves a large $-12.14$-point spot estimate but less episode-level precision ($p=0.059$); releasing after three increases expands the state to 191 months and attenuates the estimate to $-3.59$ ($p=0.161$). Replacing the 2-year yield with a 3-month rate produces $-4.68$ ($p=0.146$). The S\&P-beta spot estimates remain negative and statistically detectable under both perturbations. The state is therefore more successful at locating an exposure-ordered currency condition than at delivering an invariant carry magnitude.

The raw current-inversion count supplies a second benchmark. Its carry spot coefficient is $-3.18$ ($p=0.047$), compared with $-12.85$ for the live state. Weighting current inversions by reciprocal age revives the coefficient to $-10.43$ ($p=0.040$) and produces a $-14.87$ S\&P-beta estimate ($p=0.001$). Freshness contains information. The live release rule adds a different timing margin by remaining active as curves begin to re-steepen; that margin also retains several historically large loss months. This is exactly why the public rule-family audit in Section~\ref{sec:robustness_limits} matters.

\subsection{Synchronization versus nearby curve states}

The portfolio horse race tests whether the configuration adds information beyond the displayed alternatives. The first joint specification includes the continuous average G10 10Y--2Y slope, its one-month change, and the U.S. inversion. The synchronized-state coefficients remain $-13.45$ for carry spot and $-12.44$ for total returns, with episode-bootstrap $p=0.003$ and 0.012. The average-slope variables are imprecise in the carry columns, and the U.S. inversion enters positively and imprecisely.

Adding all nine country-level live states makes the synchronized coefficient more negative: $-26.79$ for carry spot and $-27.52$ for total returns. The nine own states are jointly informative as well. The larger cluster coefficient is not a causal purification. The related regressors divide variation from the same curves, and the own states absorb many benign single-country months. The appropriate conclusion is that the cross-country configuration contains information not spanned by the reported continuous, U.S., or own-state transformations.

The U.S. inversion alone reinforces the distinction. It is active in 57 lagged months and has carry spot and total coefficients of $-1.90$ ($p=0.484$) and $-1.34$ ($p=0.637$). Adding the U.S. curve to the cluster count, however, leaves the baseline result nearly unchanged. The U.S. curve is neither a substitute for the international configuration nor a reason to treat the United States as economically irrelevant.
