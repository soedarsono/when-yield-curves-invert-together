\section{Robustness and Limits}
\label{sec:robustness_limits}

The robustness evidence answers three questions. The median, lower quantiles, and bottom-decile deletion show that the return gap extends beyond a few crisis observations. Raw inversion breadth, the U.S. curve, the continuous G10 slope, NFCI, and VIX do not reproduce the result in the reported specifications. Carry coefficients remain negative in both calendar halves and within roughly one-third of the full-sample estimate when any one episode is removed.

An exploratory public-data screen of all two- and three-country delivered-easing combinations produces one family-adjusted CHF--GBP lead, but it weakens under stricter event-count rules and after omitting October 2008. Online Appendix~E therefore treats it as a sensor-versus-risk-bearer hypothesis, not a country-driver result.

The exact-one versus exact-two contrast isolates synchronization within the sample. The high-beta portfolio and country gradient support exposure ordering, although the panel interaction is imprecise. Replacing 10Y--2Y slopes with 10Y--3M slopes or removing a U.S. term-premium component attenuates the carry estimate but preserves a statistically detectable high-beta loss. Exposure ordering is less sensitive to these perturbations than the baseline carry magnitude. Conditional UIP and industrial production are imprecise; emerging-market breadth and the downcurve split are exploratory.

Specification selection remains the main uncertainty. The tenor, two-curve threshold, and release rule were assessed on the same return history. A deterministic real-time algorithm can overfit without fitted coefficients. Neighboring-rule grids show whether the baseline is isolated, but cannot exhaust discretion over tenors, cutoffs, countries, freshness, persistence, portfolio size, and timing.

Fifteen episodes limit independent common-state variation. Money-market accounting is not an executable forward strategy after basis, spreads, and timing costs. Predetermined equity beta discriminates among patterns but does not instrument for global growth. U.S. ACM components cannot decompose foreign term premia. Month-end dating prevents mechanical look-ahead but does not audit asynchronous closes, stale quotes, or historical vintages.

The result is a large in-sample predictive association ordered by rates and predetermined equity exposure. It does not establish causality or deployable performance. A frozen sample and feasible forward returns provide the next tests.
